Intranet · Public Website · CMS · Content Strategy 2018 to 2024

Ohio State Website Migration

The Ohio State University, Moritz College of Law

Role Web Management Specialist
Scope Strategy · CMS build · Content · Accessibility · Ongoing management
Tools WordPress · CMS · Google Analytics
The Challenge

An outdated, hard-coded web platform was creating friction for users and holding back conversion on both the public college website and the internal staff and student intranet. Content updates were slow, inconsistent, and dependent on technical staff, and the transition had to happen with no downtime and no content loss.

Ranked #34 nationally, Moritz College of Law needed platforms that matched its standing: a public website that competed for prospective students, and an intranet that actually served the people who used it every day.

The Brief

Platform Migration: Move both the public site and the legacy intranet to a flexible, maintainable CMS.

Information Architecture: Redesign the intranet around two distinct user groups (Students vs. Staff and Faculty) and rebuild the public site around conversion and discoverability.

Brand Governance: Build a self-serve communications toolkit so individual departments could produce on-brand materials without relying on the central design team.

What Was Built

1. Public Website Redesign

The redesign replaced a dense three-column text layout and a static building photo with an editorial, people-first design. The new site leads with a full-width faculty story and a clear call to action, and a magazine-style card grid surfaces news and events instead of burying them. Navigation was simplified, and the whole structure moved into the CMS, giving the team editorial control they had not had before.

2. Audience-Segmented Intranet

The intranet homepage was built around two distinct user paths, so visitors could choose their journey immediately.

For Students: A scannable dashboard where curriculum planning, directories, and common request forms became visual icon cards, greatly reducing clicks for the most frequent actions.

For Staff & Faculty: A dedicated administrative hub covering HR, IT, grant applications, and committee access, with a persistent, searchable directory.

3. Self-Serve Design Toolkit

To remove the communication bottleneck, I built a central branded asset hub. Every department could download print and digital templates, curated image libraries, and logo files, so non-designers could produce consistent, on-brand materials on their own and the central team’s backlog disappeared.

My Contribution
6-Year Tenure
  • Full project lifecycle: Managed end-to-end delivery, aligning cross-functional requirements, coordinating stakeholders, overseeing the CMS build, and ensuring no content was lost during migration.
  • UX & IA strategy: Defined the audience segmentation, navigation hierarchy, and content taxonomy for both the public site and the intranet.
  • Accessibility compliance: Built and maintained all CMS page templates to strict accessibility standards during the project and after launch.
  • Operational management: Coordinated content updates, production schedules, and cross-departmental requests over six years, and managed access permissions and distribution lists.
Result

Delivered on time with zero downtime and no content loss. In the first quarter after launch, the new public site showed measurable UX improvements and conversion gains, and prospective student enquiries increased. The CMS migration gave the college full editorial independence, and the self-serve toolkit nearly eliminated ad hoc template requests, keeping the brand consistent across all college channels.

Intranet · UX Research · Content Strategy 2017 to 2018

Enterprise Intranet Redesign

Nationwide Insurance

Role Intranet Strategy Intern
Scope User research · IA · Content planning
Tools Miro · Surveys · User Interviews
The Challenge

The Nationwide enterprise intranet followed an organisation-centric model, with navigation mapped to internal business units. It reflected the company structure, but it made information hard to find for employees whose daily tasks crossed departmental lines.

With resources spread across disconnected SharePoint sites and no central planning process, information was often buried, leading to a steady rise in avoidable support tickets.

The Brief

Research: Map employee mental models to find where the org-chart logic failed daily workflows.

Information Architecture: Evolve the system into a task-based IA that prioritises the most frequent employee actions.

Systems Design: Develop a sustainable editorial calendar in Miro to coordinate communications across six internal channels.

What Was Built

1. Task-Based Information Architecture

Mapped in Miro, the new IA moved away from departmental silos toward user intent. Based on interviews and survey data, I restructured the site into two high-level paths: “For Employees,” a fast lane for frequent tasks like payroll, benefits, and HR forms, and “For Managers,” a dedicated space for cross-functional planning and team resources. A persistent Quick Links panel surfaced the most common requests, cutting the path to key resources from four clicks to two.

2. Cross-Channel Editorial System

To fix the scattered content problem, I designed an operational calendar in Miro. It replaced an ad hoc email process with a shared source of truth for the communications team, coordinating newsletters, town halls, and intranet posts, with a modular template the team could update monthly without design software expertise.

My Contribution
  • Discovery: Conducted 12 in-depth interviews and an 84-person survey to quantify navigation friction and map employee mental models.
  • Stakeholder management: Presented research-backed IA recommendations to IT and Communications leads to secure buy-in for implementation.
  • Design systems: Built the Miro editorial framework with colour-coded content categories and accessibility-compliant (WCAG 2.1) visual cues.
  • Technical validation: Partnered with IT to make sure the new structure worked within existing CMS constraints.
Result

The task-based IA was adopted as the new enterprise standard. By aligning the intranet with how employees actually worked, the redesign addressed the root cause of the findability gap, not just the symptoms. During my internship, the editorial calendar moved into regular monthly use, and follow-up data showed a clear improvement in resource discoverability.

Strategy · Data Analysis · Excel 2024 to 2025

EV Charging Network, Operational Intelligence Report

BayWa Mobility Solutions × LMU Munich

Role Student Consultant, Data Analysis
Scope Data modelling · Pivot analysis · Dashboard · Strategy support
Tools Excel · VLOOKUP · Conditional Formatting · Pivot Charts
Context

This project was part of a student consulting engagement for BayWa Mobility Solutions, a major player in Germany’s EV transition with 500+ charging locations and a significant share of the country’s DC fast-charging infrastructure. The team’s strategy work, including competitive analysis, customer journey mapping, and a SWOT analysis, explored why drivers were not returning. My focus was turning raw charging logs into a decision-support tool for the operations team.

EV operators often have large amounts of session data but no structured way to see daily grid impact or the cost of energy at the moment of delivery. I designed this workbook as a reusable framework to evaluate performance across AC, DC Fast Charging (DCFC), and High Power Charging (HPC) assets.

The Brief

Develop a prototype reporting workbook for a 6-month performance review. The tool needed to track station utilisation, pinpoint grid load spikes (the duck curve), and model the impact of Time-of-Use (TOU) pricing, in a format the permanent operations team could take over.

What Was Built

Operational Dashboard: Load Profile & Efficiency

An executive view with a Utilisation vs. Target analysis and a duck curve chart. The curve shows hourly load, colour-coded by electricity price band (Off-Peak vs. On-Peak), making it clear how much energy is sold during the most expensive grid windows.

Dynamic TOU Rate Engine

A Raw Data sheet where every charging session is priced automatically with a VLOOKUP (approximate match), pulling the electricity cost from a rate table based on the session’s start hour. This replaced what had been a manual estimate.

My Contribution
  • Unified data model: Consolidated session logs from station types with different power outputs and session lengths into one comparable structure.
  • Automated cost modelling: Built the VLOOKUP logic, =VLOOKUP(HOUR(timestamp), rate_table, 2, TRUE), to calculate Time-of-Use costs per session automatically.
  • Demand window analysis: Aggregated 6 months of data and found that 67% of energy demand fell in the 18:00 to 20:00 window, giving a data-backed case for peak shaving.
  • Operational handover: Designed the workbook with protected formulas and clear input areas so the team could keep using it independently after the engagement.
Result

The workbook showed the operations team which stations were reaching their 65% utilisation target and where electricity surcharges were squeezing margins. The duck curve confirmed that 67% of energy demand fell within the on-peak TOU window, a concrete case for investing in peak shaving. The formula logic extends to more stations or pricing zones without structural changes, so the tool stays useful beyond the engagement.
